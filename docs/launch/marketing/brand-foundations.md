## 1. Brand personality

**In one line:** the straight-talking engineer from down the road who builds things that work and tells you what
they can't do yet.

| Trait | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Plain-spoken | Short sentences, concrete nouns (register, machine, shift, row) | Dumbed down, slangy |
| Honest about limits | States what is built, in progress and planned, every time | Self-deprecating or apologetic |
| Practical | Every message ends in something the reader can do | Feature lists for their own sake |
| Respectful of the owner | Assumes the reader runs a business and knows it better than us | Lecturing about "digital transformation" |
| Local and bilingual | Comfortable in Hindi, Hinglish and English; examples from Indian plants | Caricatured "desi" humour |
| Quietly competent | Shows real screens and lets them speak | Superlatives, hype, "revolutionary" |

Archetype (for designers): the **craftsman/engineer**, not the magician or the hero.

## 2. Voice and tone

### Voice rules (always)

1. Say the thing in the first sentence.
2. Use the reader's words: "register", "rejection", "breakdown", "indent", not "workflow orchestration".
3. One idea per sentence; under 20 words where possible.
4. Numbers only when measured and sourced; examples always labelled "example".
5. Every feature claim matches [BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md). Planned features carry the word "planned".
6. Never: "secure", "bank-grade", "certified", "compliant", "AI-powered", "world-class", "#1", "trusted by",
   "seamless", "revolutionary", "next-gen", "unlock", "empower".
7. Technical terms stay in English inside Hindi/Hinglish text (app, backup, formula, server, login).

### Do / don't

| Situation | Don't | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | "Empower your enterprise with next-gen low-code" | "Your registers, as apps, on your own computer" |
| Security | "Bank-grade security keeps your data safe" | "Each role sees only its rows and columns. Check it with Test as user." |
| Roadmap | "WhatsApp alerts for every breakdown" | "WhatsApp alerts: planned. Today, alerts arrive in the app and by email." |
| Proof | "Trusted by manufacturers across India" | "Version 0.5. Looking for three design partners near Bhopal." |
| Comparison | "Power Apps is overpriced and complicated" | "Power Apps Premium is US$20 per user per month, US$22 from January 2027. If you already use Microsoft 365 heavily, it may suit you better." |
| Error message (product) | "Oops! Something went wrong 😕" | "The save was refused: Quantity must be more than 0." |
| WhatsApp | "Dear Sir/Madam, greetings of the day!!! Kindly find..." | "Namaste Sharma ji, yeh raha OEE calculator ka link. Band karne ke liye STOP likhein." |
| Hinglish | Forced slang ("Ekdum jhakaas app, boss!") | Natural speech ("Supervisor sirf apni line ka data dekhega.") |

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Ads and landing pages | Direct, specific, one benefit, one honest qualifier |
| Founder posts | First person, reflective, shows work in progress |
| Docs and product text | Calm, exact, instructional |
| Sales conversations | Curious, question-led, explicit about gaps |
| Partners | Peer-to-peer, technical, frank about margins and maturity |
| Incidents / bugs | Fast, factual, accountable ("This was our bug. Here's what we changed.") |

### Language rules

- Social, WhatsApp, video voice-over: Hinglish in Roman script by default; Devanagari for print and posters.
- Website: English first, Hindi pages for the manufacturing landing pages and templates.
- Hindi register: everyday Hindi (Hindustani), not Sanskritised. "Upyog karein" is fine in print; "use karein" in
  WhatsApp.
- Honorifics: "ji" with surnames or first names for owners; "aap" always.

## 3. Naming criteria (shared with the launch-plan workstream)

Must:
1. Coined or arbitrary, not descriptive (descriptive names like "AppBuilder" or "Low Code India" are weak trademarks).
2. Clear in India in Nice classes 9 (software) and 42 (SaaS/IT services); later UK and UAE (checks by the lawyer).
3. Pronounceable the same way in Hindi and English; spelled correctly after hearing it once on the phone.
4. Writable cleanly in Devanagari.
5. No negative or embarrassing meaning in Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil, Bengali, Urdu/Arabic or English slang
   (native-speaker check).
6. A usable domain (.com preferred; .in acceptable for India phase) and matching handles on LinkedIn, YouTube,
   Instagram, X.
7. 2-3 syllables, 4-8 letters preferred.

Must not:
- Use or echo Microsoft or Tally marks ("Power…", "…Apps" + "Power", "Tally…").
- Be confusable with existing Indian SME software brands (for example names built on "khata" sit close to Khatabook).
- End in "-ify"/"-ly" by default (crowded); allowed only if the rest is distinctive.
- Depend on a pun that only works in one language.

## 4. Brand territories (3) with example names (ALL UNCHECKED)

| Territory | Idea | Feels like | Example names (UNCHECKED) | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The workshop** | Craft, tools, the shop floor; "built by people who make things" | Steel, ochre, stencil type, the sound of a lathe | Karigo (from *karigar*, craftsman), Tolka, Anvo, Forja | Can feel manufacturing-only, which limits ICP 3 later; Spanish/Portuguese "forja" is a common word |
| **B. Yours, at home** | Ownership; data and apps stay on your premises; "apna" | Warm, domestic, trustworthy, a locked almirah | Apnora (from *apna*, own), Gharu (from *ghar*, home), Ownstead, Keepa | "Apna"-based names are common in Indian brands (**likely crowded**); "home" may suggest consumer products |
| **C. The register, reborn** | The bahi-khata and register book as heritage; order, lines, clarity | Cream paper, ink blue, ruled lines, a fountain-pen stroke | Panno (from *panna*, page), Rekha-based coinages (Rekhan), Bahiq, Registra | Must avoid "khata"-space confusion; "Rekha" is a personal name; "Registra" is close to "registrar" |

Recommendation (**Inference**): territory C fits the wedge (registers → apps) and works for ICP 3 and global markets
too ("register" is understood everywhere); territory A is the strongest for ICP 1 alone; territory B is the most
emotionally resonant but most crowded. Run the first trademark search on 3 names from C and 2 from A.

## 6. Visual direction (3 routes, described in words)

All routes: real product screenshots are the hero; sample data visibly fake; open-licence fonts with Devanagari
support (for example Inter or IBM Plex Sans with Noto Sans Devanagari or Mukta, all SIL Open Font Licence); accessible
contrast (WCAG AA); no stock "happy office" photography; people only as illustrations or labelled models.

### Route 1: "Shop-floor honest"
- **Palette:** steel grey (#3A3F44 range), concrete (#D9D6CF), safety ochre/yellow accent (#E3A21A range), a deep
  machine green for success states.
- **Type:** a sturdy grotesk for headlines (condensed weight), stencil-inspired numerals only for big figures.
- **Imagery:** daylight photos of real-looking factory offices, register books, machine panels, gloves; product
  screens on laptops placed in those settings.
- **Graphic device:** hazard-tape diagonal used sparingly as a "planned" marker.
- **Fits:** ICP 1, print flyers, trade events. **Risk:** looks like an industrial-equipment brand; weaker for ICP 2/3.

### Route 2: "Register reborn"
- **Palette:** cream paper (#F6F1E7), ink blue (#1F3A8A range), red ruling line accent (#C0392B range, used thinly),
  graphite.
- **Type:** a humanist sans for UI-like text plus a warm serif for headlines; Devanagari pairing designed as equals.
- **Imagery:** ruled register pages whose lines continue into the product's table grid; hand-written entries becoming
  typed rows.
- **Graphic device:** the "ruled line" that runs through layouts and becomes a table border.
- **Fits:** all ICPs, works globally, supports territory C names. **Risk:** can look nostalgic if overused; keep
  screens modern.

### Route 3: "Quiet software"
- **Palette:** continues the current product UI: white and light grey surfaces, indigo primary (as in the product's
  buttons and selection), a red "PRODUCTION" badge colour, green "succeeded" chips.
- **Type:** Inter (as product-style UI), tight hierarchy.
- **Imagery:** product screens only, large, cropped to one idea each; subtle device frames.
- **Graphic device:** status chips (Built / In progress / Planned) as a recurring element.
- **Fits:** ICP 2/3, SEO pages, global phase; cheapest to produce. **Risk:** generic SaaS look; little warmth for
  ICP 1.

**Recommendation:** Route 2 as the brand, with Route 3 rules for product-led assets (docs, comparison pages, partner
material). Use Route 1 elements only for event print in industrial areas. Final choice after the name, since the name
territory and the visual route should match.

## 7. Messaging by audience

| Audience | Core message | Proof today | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICP 1 owner | "Your registers as apps, on your own computer, with who-sees-what built in." | Real screens; on-site build session; backups | Talking about "platform", "low-code" |
| ICP 1 plant head / supervisor | "Enter once, everyone who should see it sees it; nobody else." | Roles and Test as user; validation rules | Promising phone entry (planned) |
| ICP 2 consultant | "Canvas apps with Excel-style formulas your clients host themselves." | Formula bar, environments, security, installers; honest status list | "Power Fx compatible"; price comparisons before pricing exists |
| ICP 3 ops generalist | "Your 14 Excel files, one system, permissions included." | Start with data, scheduled reports, tasks and comments | "Replaces all your SaaS tools" |
| CAs / Tally partners | "Something useful to offer clients for the work outside accounts." | Scorecard, checklist, clinic | Anything that sounds like accounting/GST software (ERP ledgers are planned, not built) |
| Global (Phase D) | "A self-hosted business app platform for small organisations." | Docs, public download (when available) | India-specific references in global pages |
